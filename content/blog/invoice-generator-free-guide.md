## What Makes a Professional Invoice?

A complete, professional invoice includes:

### Your Business Information
- Business or full name
- Address
- Email and phone number
- VAT or tax registration number (if applicable)

### Client Information
- Client name and business name
- Client billing address
- Contact email

### Invoice Details
- Unique invoice number (for your records and their accounting)
- Invoice date
- Payment due date
- Currency

### Line Items
Each product or service you are billing for, with:
- Description
- Quantity
- Unit price
- Line total (quantity × price)

### Totals
- Subtotal (before tax)
- Tax amount (VAT, GST, sales tax — as applicable)
- Grand total due

### Payment Terms
How you accept payment: bank transfer (with account details), PayPal, credit card, or other. This is where you include your IBAN/SWIFT if billing internationally.

## Invoice Numbering Best Practices

Use a consistent numbering system so you can track invoices easily:

- **Sequential:** INV-001, INV-002, INV-003
- **Date-based:** INV-2026-001, INV-2026-002
- **Client-based:** SMITH-001, SMITH-002

Never reuse invoice numbers. Each invoice must have a unique number for both your accounting and your client's.

## Payment Terms to Include on Every Invoice

Clear payment terms reduce late payments significantly:

- **Net 7** — payment due within 7 days
- **Net 14** — payment due within 14 days
- **Net 30** — standard for B2B (most common)
- **Due on Receipt** — payment expected immediately

Adding a small **late fee clause** (e.g., "1.5% per month on overdue balances") also encourages prompt payment.

## When Do You Need an Invoice vs. a Receipt?

An **invoice** is sent before or when requesting payment — it is a request for money.
A **receipt** is issued after payment is received — it confirms the money was paid.

Most clients need an invoice first; they may also ask for a receipt after paying. Our tool generates the invoice; you can also mark it as "Paid" to send it as a receipt.
