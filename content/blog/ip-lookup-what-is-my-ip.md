## What Is an IP Address?

IP stands for **Internet Protocol**. An IP address is the unique number that identifies your device (or network) on the internet. There are two formats in active use:

### IPv4
The traditional format: four groups of numbers separated by dots.

**Example:** `192.168.1.1` (local/private)
**Example:** `203.0.113.47` (public)

IPv4 supports about 4.3 billion unique addresses — a number that has been exhausted globally. Network techniques like NAT (Network Address Translation) allow many devices to share a single public IPv4 address.

### IPv6
The newer format: eight groups of four hexadecimal digits separated by colons.

**Example:** `2001:0db8:85a3:0000:0000:8a2e:0370:7334`

IPv6 supports 340 undecillion addresses (3.4 × 10^38) — essentially unlimited. Most modern devices and networks support both IPv4 and IPv6.

## Public IP vs Private IP — What Is the Difference?

### Public IP Address
This is the IP address that the internet sees when you connect. It is assigned by your Internet Service Provider (ISP) and is shared across all devices on your home or office network. When you visit a website, the site sees your public IP.

### Private IP Address
This is the IP address assigned to your specific device within your local network (home WiFi, office LAN). Private IPs follow reserved ranges:
- `10.0.0.0 – 10.255.255.255`
- `172.16.0.0 – 172.31.255.255`
- `192.168.0.0 – 192.168.255.255`

Your router assigns private IPs to each device and translates between private and public addresses using NAT.

### How Accurate Is IP Geolocation?

Country-level accuracy is typically 95–99%. City-level accuracy drops to 50–80%. The location shown is not your exact address — it is usually the city or region where your ISP routes traffic, which may differ from your physical location, especially with mobile data or VPNs.

IP addresses do **not** reveal:

- Your name or personal identity
- Your browsing history

### Security Monitoring

## Static vs Dynamic IP Addresses

Most home internet connections use **dynamic IPs** — your ISP assigns a different IP each time you connect or after a lease period. This means your IP may change periodically.

**Static IPs** stay the same and are typically offered as a paid add-on by ISPs for businesses that need a stable address for hosting servers, remote access, or IP-based security.

If you notice your IP changes frequently, you have a dynamic IP. This is normal and expected for residential connections.

## Does Using a VPN or Proxy Change My IP?

Yes. A VPN routes your traffic through a server in another location, and websites see the VPN server's IP instead of yours. This is the primary purpose of VPNs for privacy. Our IP lookup tool shows whichever IP your connection is currently using, making it a convenient way to verify VPN status.

### Why does my IP show a different city than where I live?
IP geolocation uses ISP registration data, not your actual location. Your ISP may route traffic through a hub in a different city, causing the reported location to differ. This is normal and expected. Geolocation is an estimate, not GPS tracking.

### Can someone find my home address from my IP?
No. IP addresses reveal the ISP and approximate city but not your physical home address. Only your ISP has the records linking your IP to your specific account and address — and law enforcement needs a court order to obtain that.

### How do I hide my IP address?
Use a VPN (Virtual Private Network). A VPN routes your traffic through a server in a location of your choice, and websites see the VPN server's IP instead of yours. You can verify it is working by checking your IP with our lookup tool before and after connecting to the VPN.
