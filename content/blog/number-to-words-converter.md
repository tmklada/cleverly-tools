## Why Convert Numbers to Words?

### Checks and Bank Drafts
When writing a physical check, you must spell out the dollar amount in words on the written line. This is a legal requirement — the written amount takes precedence over the numeric amount if they conflict. Getting it wrong can get your check rejected.

**Example:** $1,250.50 → "One thousand two hundred fifty and 50/100 dollars"

### Legal and Formal Documents
Contracts, deeds, and notarized documents often require numbers spelled out to prevent alteration. A contract might read: "The fee shall be five thousand dollars ($5,000)."

### Invoices with Tax Requirements
Some tax jurisdictions require invoice amounts written in words. In many countries, a VAT invoice must include the total in text form.

### Exams and Education
Spelling out numbers correctly is a tested skill in many standardized exams and language certifications.

### International Communication
When there is risk of misreading digits (especially with comma vs period as decimal separator across countries), writing numbers in words eliminates ambiguity.

### Accessibility
Screen readers handle written numbers more reliably than digits in certain contexts — particularly for currency and large numbers.

## How to Write Numbers in Words — Rules

### Cardinal Numbers (Counting)

**1–19:** one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, nineteen

**Tens:** twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety

**Compound:** hyphenate twenty-one through ninety-nine: twenty-one, forty-seven, ninety-nine

**Hundreds:** "one hundred," "two hundred fifty-three" — note: no "and" in American English, "and" is accepted in British English before the tens/ones ("two hundred and fifty-three")

**Thousands and above:** "one thousand," "ten thousand," "one hundred thousand," "one million," "one billion"

### Ordinal Numbers (Ordering)
Add -st, -nd, -rd, or -th to the cardinal: first, second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth... twenty-first, forty-second, one hundredth

### Currency in Words (Checks)
Format: "Amount and cents/100 dollars" or "Amount dollars and cents cents"

**$1,234.56 →** "One thousand two hundred thirty-four and 56/100 dollars"

**$0.99 →** "Ninety-nine cents" or "Zero and 99/100 dollars"

## Large Number Names

| Number | Name |
|--------|------|
| 1,000 | One thousand |
| 1,000,000 | One million |
| 1,000,000,000 | One billion |
| 1,000,000,000,000 | One trillion |
| 10^15 | One quadrillion |
| 10^18 | One quintillion |

Note: In the British "long scale" system (used in some European countries), a billion traditionally meant 10^12 (a million million), not 10^9. Modern international usage follows the American "short scale" where billion = 10^9.

## FAQ

### How do I write a check amount in words?
Write the dollar amount in words, then the cents as a fraction over 100. Example: $1,250.75 → "One thousand two hundred fifty and 75/100 dollars." Use our [Number to Words Converter](https://cleverly.tools/number-to-words) and select the "Currency" format to get the exact check-ready format.

### Is there a difference between American and British number-to-words?
Yes. In American English: "one hundred twenty-three." In British English: "one hundred and twenty-three" — note the "and" before the tens. Also, Americans say "a billion" for 10^9, while the British long scale traditionally used "a thousand million" — though modern British usage has adopted the American short scale.

### How do you spell out very large numbers?
Use the scale: thousand (10^3), million (10^6), billion (10^9), trillion (10^12), quadrillion (10^15). For example: 5,000,000,000 = "five billion." Our converter handles numbers up to and beyond 10^18.
