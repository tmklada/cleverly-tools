## What Is an IP Address?

IP stands for **Internet Protocol**. An IP address is a numerical label assigned to every device on a network. Think of it like a postal address — it tells other computers on the internet where to send data back to when you request a webpage, send an email, or stream a video.

There are two versions of IP addresses currently in use:

| Version | Format | Example | Status |
|---------|--------|---------|--------|
| IPv4 | Four numbers (0–255) separated by dots | 192.168.1.1 | Still dominant, but running out |
| IPv6 | Eight groups of four hex digits | 2001:0db8:85a3:0000:0000:8a2e:0370:7334 | Growing adoption, long-term future |

## Public IP vs. Private IP Address

There's an important distinction between two types of IP addresses:

### Public IP Address
This is the address your internet service provider (ISP) assigns to your router. It's visible to the outside internet — every website you visit can see your public IP. This is what our [IP Address Lookup](/tools/ip-address-lookup) tool shows you.

### Private IP Address
This is the address your router assigns to each device on your home network (192.168.x.x or 10.0.x.x). Only devices on your local network can see these — they're not visible from the internet.

**Example:** Your router has one public IP (e.g., 72.43.156.90). Your laptop, phone, and smart TV each have different private IPs (192.168.1.2, 192.168.1.3, 192.168.1.4), but they all share the same public IP when browsing the internet.

## What Information Is Visible Through Your IP Address?

When a website or service sees your IP address, they can typically determine:

| Information | How Accurate |
|-------------|-------------|
| Country | Very accurate (99%+) |
| Region / State | Accurate (~80–90%) |
| City | Roughly accurate (~70%) — often the ISP's city, not yours |
| ISP / Internet Provider | Very accurate |
| Connection type | Usually accurate (residential, mobile, VPN, datacenter) |
| Precise home address | NOT possible — this is a common myth |

Your exact home address, name, and personal details are **not** visible through your IP address alone. That information requires a court order to the ISP.

## Static vs. Dynamic IP Address

| Type | What It Means | Who Gets It |
|------|--------------|-------------|
| Static IP | Same IP address every time you connect | Businesses, servers, some advanced plans |
| Dynamic IP | IP address changes periodically | Most home internet users |

Most residential internet connections use **dynamic IPs** that change when you restart your router or periodically on your ISP's schedule. This is normal and not a problem for regular browsing.

## Why Would You Need to Know Your IP Address?

Common reasons to check your IP address:

- **Troubleshooting internet issues** — your ISP support team may ask for your IP
- **Setting up remote access** — configuring VPN, SSH, or remote desktop needs your IP
- **Whitelisting your IP** — some services allow access only from approved IP addresses
- **Gaming and servers** — hosting a game server or playing peer-to-peer games often requires your public IP
- **Verifying VPN connection** — check that your VPN is working by confirming a different IP is shown
- **Security monitoring** — seeing if your IP has changed unexpectedly

## How to Change or Hide Your IP Address

If you want to change or mask your IP address:

- **Restart your router** — may assign a new dynamic IP (not guaranteed)
- **Use a VPN** — routes traffic through a VPN server, so websites see that server's IP instead of yours
- **Use Tor Browser** — routes traffic through multiple nodes for strong anonymity
- **Mobile data** — switching from WiFi to your phone's data connection uses a different IP

For privacy reasons, many people use a VPN. After connecting, use our [IP Address Lookup](/tools/ip-address-lookup) to verify that your IP and location have changed.

## FAQ — IP Address Lookup

**Can someone find my home address using my IP address?**
No — not without a court order. Your public IP address can reveal your approximate city and your ISP (internet provider), but not your home address or personal identity. Only your ISP has the records linking your IP to your account, and they only release that under legal compulsion.

**Why does my IP address show the wrong city?**
IP geolocation is an approximation. The city shown is often the location of your ISP's nearest data center or routing hub, not your actual city. For most residential connections, the location is accurate to the state or region level but may show the wrong specific city.

**Why do I have two IP addresses — IPv4 and IPv6?**
Many internet connections now support both IPv4 and IPv6 simultaneously (called "dual-stack"). If you see both on the IP lookup tool, it means your ISP supports both protocols and your device is using them. This is normal and increasingly common.

**Does my phone have a different IP than my laptop?**
Yes — when on mobile data, your phone has a completely different public IP assigned by your mobile carrier. When both devices are on the same WiFi network, they share the same public IP but have different private IPs assigned by your router.
