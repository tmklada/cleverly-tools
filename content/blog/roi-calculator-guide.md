# ROI Calculator — How to Calculate Return on Investment

Every business decision involves spending money to get something back. ROI — Return on Investment — tells you whether that exchange was worth it. It is one of the most universally used financial metrics because it works for everything: marketing campaigns, equipment purchases, hiring decisions, and stock investments.

## What Is ROI?

ROI measures the profitability of an investment relative to its cost. It answers the question: **for every dollar I spent, how much did I get back?**

A positive ROI means you came out ahead. A negative ROI means you lost money on the investment. A zero ROI means you broke even.

## The ROI Formula

**ROI = (Net Profit / Cost of Investment) × 100**

Or equivalently:

**ROI = ((Return − Cost) / Cost) × 100**

The result is expressed as a percentage.

**Example:** You spend $2,000 on a Facebook ad campaign. It generates $5,500 in sales.
- Net Profit = $5,500 − $2,000 = $3,500
- ROI = ($3,500 / $2,000) × 100 = **175%**

That means for every $1 spent, you earned $1.75 in profit on top of your investment.

## When to Use ROI

### Marketing and Advertising
ROI is the gold standard for evaluating campaigns. If you run ads on Google, Meta, or TikTok, your ROI tells you which channel delivers the best return so you can allocate budget more effectively.

### Business Purchases
Before buying software, equipment, or property, calculate the expected ROI. If a $10,000 machine will save $4,000 per year in labor, your payback period is 2.5 years and your 5-year ROI is 100%.

### Hiring and Training
Investing in an employee or a training program has an ROI too. Estimate the value they will produce and compare it against salary, onboarding, and training costs.

### Personal Finance and Investing
Stocks, real estate, and side businesses all have ROI. Comparing them on a common percentage basis helps you decide where to put your money.

## How to Calculate ROI Step by Step

### Step 1 — Define what counts as "investment cost"
Include everything: purchase price, setup costs, maintenance, time spent (valued at an hourly rate), and any indirect expenses.

### Step 2 — Define what counts as "return"
This is revenue, savings, or value generated directly attributable to the investment. Be conservative — do not include revenue that would have happened anyway.

### Step 3 — Calculate net profit
Net Profit = Total Return − Total Cost

### Step 4 — Divide and multiply
ROI = (Net Profit / Total Cost) × 100

### Step 5 — Interpret the result
- **ROI > 0%** — profitable investment
- **ROI = 0%** — broke even
- **ROI < 0%** — lost money
- **Compare ROI across options** to rank the best use of capital

## Limitations of ROI

ROI does not account for time. An investment that returns 50% over 10 years is far less attractive than one that returns 50% over 6 months. For time-sensitive comparisons, use **annualized ROI** or metrics like IRR (Internal Rate of Return).

ROI also does not capture risk. Two investments with the same ROI are not equally good if one is riskier.

## FAQ

### What is a good ROI percentage?
It depends on the type of investment. Marketing campaigns often target 200–400% ROI. Stock market investments average about 7–10% annually. Real estate varies by market. The key benchmark is whether your ROI exceeds the cost of capital — if borrowing money at 8% to fund an investment, you need ROI above 8% to profit.

### What is the difference between ROI and profit margin?
Profit margin measures profit as a percentage of revenue. ROI measures profit as a percentage of the investment cost. They answer different questions: margin tells you how efficiently you convert sales to profit; ROI tells you how efficiently you deploy capital.

### Can ROI be negative?
Yes. A negative ROI means the investment returned less than it cost. For example, spending $1,000 on a campaign that brings in $700 in revenue gives an ROI of −30%.

### Does ROI include time?
The basic ROI formula does not factor in time. If you need to compare investments with different durations, calculate annualized ROI: Annualized ROI = ((1 + ROI)^(1/years) − 1) × 100.
