# Body Fat Calculator — How to Calculate Your Body Fat %

Your weight on a scale tells you very little. Two people can weigh exactly the same and look completely different — because what matters isn't total weight, it's **body composition**: how much of your body is fat versus lean mass (muscle, bone, water, organs).

Body fat percentage is a far more accurate health metric than weight or even BMI. Our free [Body Fat Calculator](https://cleverly.tools/body-fat-calculator) estimates your body fat % using the U.S. Navy Method — accurate, proven, and requires only a tape measure.

## What Is Body Fat Percentage?

Body fat percentage is the proportion of your total body weight that is fat. Everyone needs some fat to survive — it cushions organs, regulates hormones, and stores energy. The question is how much fat is healthy versus excess.

**Healthy body fat ranges by category:**

| Category | Women | Men |
|----------|-------|-----|
| Essential fat | 10–13% | 2–5% |
| Athletic | 14–20% | 6–13% |
| Fitness | 21–24% | 14–17% |
| Acceptable | 25–31% | 18–24% |
| Obese | 32%+ | 25%+ |

## How the Navy Method Works

The U.S. Navy Body Fat Formula was developed in the 1980s and has been used by the military for decades. It uses circumference measurements at key body sites to estimate body density, then converts that to fat percentage.

**For men**, you measure:
- Neck circumference
- Waist circumference (at the navel)
- Height

**For women**, you measure:
- Neck circumference
- Waist circumference (at the narrowest point)
- Hip circumference (at the widest point)
- Height

The formula uses the difference between waist (and hips for women) and neck measurements. A larger waist relative to neck = more fat. Simple, and surprisingly accurate for most people.

## Body Fat % vs. BMI — What's the Difference?

BMI (Body Mass Index) divides your weight by height squared. It's fast and simple but blind to body composition. A muscular athlete with very low body fat can have the same BMI as an overweight sedentary person — because muscle is dense.

Body fat percentage fixes this problem. It directly measures what you care about: how much of you is fat vs. lean mass.

That said, neither metric tells the whole story. For a complete health picture, track:
- Body fat % (composition)
- BMI (general weight status)
- Waist-to-height ratio (central obesity risk)
- Visceral fat (fat around organs — the most dangerous kind)

## How to Reduce Body Fat

Losing body fat is about creating a sustained calorie deficit while preserving muscle:

**1. Eat in a moderate deficit**
A 300–500 calorie daily deficit causes about 0.5–1 lb of fat loss per week without sacrificing muscle.

**2. Prioritize protein**
Aim for 0.7–1g of protein per pound of body weight. Protein preserves muscle during a deficit and keeps you fuller longer.

**3. Strength train**
Resistance training signals your body to keep muscle even when losing fat. Without it, up to 25% of weight lost can be muscle.

**4. Do cardio you'll actually stick to**
Any cardio burns calories. The "best" cardio is the kind you do consistently.

**5. Sleep enough**
Poor sleep elevates cortisol, which promotes fat storage — especially around the belly. Use our [Sleep Calculator](https://cleverly.tools/sleep-calculator) to optimize your rest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How accurate is the Navy Method body fat calculator?**
A: The Navy Method has a margin of error of about ±3–4% compared to DEXA scans (the gold standard). It's accurate enough for tracking trends over time and categorizing your fitness level. For precise clinical measurement, a DEXA scan or hydrostatic weighing is more accurate.

**Q: How often should I check my body fat percentage?**
A: Once every 4–6 weeks is ideal. Body fat changes slowly — checking too frequently shows noise rather than real trends. Track the same conditions each time (morning, after bathroom, before eating) for the most consistent results.

**Q: Can I have a healthy body weight but high body fat?**
A: Yes — this is called "normal weight obesity" or "skinny fat." People with low muscle mass can have a healthy BMI but unhealthy body fat percentages. Strength training and adequate protein are the main solutions.

**Q: Is there a difference between subcutaneous fat and visceral fat?**
A: Yes, significantly. Subcutaneous fat sits under the skin (the fat you can pinch). Visceral fat surrounds your organs and is metabolically active — it releases inflammatory compounds and is strongly linked to heart disease, diabetes, and metabolic syndrome. A large waist circumference is the main indicator of high visceral fat.
