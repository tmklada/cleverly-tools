## What Size Should a QR Code Be?

The right size depends on where you'll use it:

### For Print
- **Business card:** minimum 2.5 cm × 2.5 cm (1 inch)
- **Flyer or poster:** 3–5 cm × 3–5 cm
- **Billboard:** at least 10 cm × 10 cm for readable scan distance

### For Digital Use
- **Website or social media:** 300 × 300 px minimum
- **Email signature:** 150 × 150 px is usually enough

## What Is Error Correction Level?

QR codes have a built-in error correction feature that lets them still scan correctly even if part of the image is damaged or covered. There are four levels:

| Level | Recovery Capability | Best For |
|-------|--------------------|-|
| L (Low) | 7% damage | Clean digital display |
| M (Medium) | 15% damage | General use |
| Q (Quartile) | 25% damage | Industrial or outdoor |
| H (High) | 30% damage | Logos overlaid on QR code |

For most use cases, **Level M** is the right balance between data density and durability.

## Can You Put a Logo Inside a QR Code?

Yes — if you use **High (H) error correction**, the QR code can still be scanned even with a logo covering up to 30% of the center. This is a popular technique for branded QR codes on packaging and marketing materials.

## Tips for Getting a Good Scan Every Time

- **Use high contrast** — dark code on a white/light background always works best
- **Add quiet zone** — leave a white border around the QR code (at least 4 modules wide)
- **Test before printing** — scan with multiple phone models before committing to print
- **Don't distort** — keep the QR code square, never stretch it

**Is this QR code generator really free?**

**Do QR codes expire?**
QR codes themselves do not expire — they're just encoded data. However, if the URL inside the QR code changes or the page goes down, the scan will fail. Static QR codes (like those generated here) are permanent as long as the destination is live.

**What's the difference between a static and dynamic QR code?**
A static QR code encodes content directly and cannot be changed after creation. A dynamic QR code links to a redirect URL that can be updated later. For most personal and small business uses, static QR codes are perfectly fine.
