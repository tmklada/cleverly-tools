## What a Professional Invoice Should Include

A valid invoice that holds up for accounting and payment purposes should contain:

| Field | Why It Matters |
|-------|---------------|
| Invoice number | Unique ID for tracking and reference |
| Invoice date | When the invoice was issued |
| Due date | Clear payment deadline |
| Your name / business name | Who is requesting payment |
| Your contact details | Email, phone, address |
| Client name and address | Who owes you money |
| Line items with description | What services or products were provided |
| Quantity and unit price | For each line item |
| Subtotal | Sum before tax |
| Tax rate and tax amount | If applicable |
| Total due | The final amount |
| Payment instructions | How to pay — bank transfer, PayPal, etc. |

Missing any of these can cause delays in getting paid or create accounting problems for your client.

## Invoice Numbering Best Practices

Keep your invoice numbers consistent and sequential. Here are common formats:

| Format | Example | Best For |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Simple sequential | INV-001, INV-002 | New freelancers |
| Year + sequence | 2026-001, 2026-002 | Annual restart |
| Client + sequence | SMITH-001, JONES-001 | Multiple regular clients |
| Date + sequence | 20260701-01 | Date-based tracking |

Consistent numbering makes it much easier to track outstanding invoices and match payments to invoices.

## Setting Payment Terms

Payment terms define when you expect to be paid. The most common options:

- **Due on receipt** — payment expected immediately upon receiving the invoice
- **Net 7** — payment due within 7 days
- **Net 15** — payment due within 15 days
- **Net 30** — payment due within 30 days (most common in business-to-business)
- **Net 60** — payment due within 60 days (larger enterprise clients often request this)

For freelancers and small businesses, **Net 7** or **Net 15** is recommended to maintain healthy cash flow. Large clients may push for Net 30 or Net 60 — be aware of this when pricing your services.

## Should You Charge Late Payment Fees?

Yes — and put it in writing on the invoice. A common clause is:

*"A late payment fee of 1.5% per month will be applied to invoices not paid within [X] days."*

Whether you enforce it or not, stating it encourages clients to pay on time. It also protects you legally if you need to pursue payment later.

## What to Do When a Client Doesn't Pay

If your invoice goes past due:

1. **Send a polite reminder** — sometimes invoices get lost or forgotten
2. **Follow up by phone** — more direct than email
3. **Send a formal overdue notice** — include the original invoice and a new due date
4. **Pause work** — stop providing services until the account is settled
5. **Collections or legal action** — for larger amounts that remain unpaid

Good invoicing practices — clear due dates, professional formatting, early reminders — reduce the chance of this happening in the first place.
