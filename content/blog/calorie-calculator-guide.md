## Two Numbers You Need to Know: BMR and TDEE

### BMR — Basal Metabolic Rate
Your BMR is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest — just to keep you alive (breathing, heart beating, maintaining body temperature). Think of it as your body's "idle" energy consumption.

### TDEE — Total Daily Energy Expenditure
TDEE is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. It's how many calories you actually burn in a day when you account for movement, exercise, and daily activity. This is the number you should use for planning your diet.

**TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier**

## Activity Level Multipliers Explained

| Activity Level | Description | Multiplier |
|---------------|-------------|------------|
| Sedentary | Desk job, little to no exercise | × 1.2 |
| Lightly Active | Light exercise 1–3 days/week | × 1.375 |
| Moderately Active | Moderate exercise 3–5 days/week | × 1.55 |
| Very Active | Hard exercise 6–7 days/week | × 1.725 |
| Extra Active | Physical job + daily training | × 1.9 |

Most people overestimate their activity level. If you sit at a desk most of the day and exercise 3 times a week, you're likely "Lightly Active" to "Moderately Active" — not "Very Active."

## Calorie Targets Based on Your Goal

Once you have your TDEE from the [Calorie Calculator](/tools/calorie-calculator), here's how to adjust it:

### To Lose Weight (Fat Loss)
Eat **300–500 calories below** your TDEE per day. This creates a deficit that leads to roughly 0.3–0.5 kg (0.5–1 lb) of fat loss per week — a safe and sustainable rate.

Avoid eating less than 1,200 calories/day (women) or 1,500 calories/day (men) without medical supervision.

### To Maintain Weight
Eat **at your TDEE**. Your weight stays stable when calories in = calories out.

### To Build Muscle (Lean Bulk)
Eat **250–500 calories above** your TDEE. Combined with strength training, this gives your body the energy surplus needed to build muscle without excessive fat gain.

## Macronutrients: Where Those Calories Come From

Calories come from three macronutrients (macros):

| Macro | Calories Per Gram | Role |
|-------|------------------|------|
| Protein | 4 kcal/g | Muscle repair, satiety, immune function |
| Carbohydrates | 4 kcal/g | Primary energy source, brain fuel |
| Fat | 9 kcal/g | Hormones, vitamin absorption, cell function |

**General macro split for most people:**
- Protein: 25–35% of total calories
- Carbohydrates: 35–50% of total calories
- Fat: 20–35% of total calories

Athletes and people focused on muscle building typically increase protein to 30–40% of calories.

## Why Calorie Counting Isn't Perfect (But Still Useful)

Calorie counts on food labels can be off by up to 20%. Your body's actual calorie burn varies day to day. And not all calories affect hunger and energy the same way — 200 calories from chicken and vegetables behaves very differently from 200 calories from candy.

Use the calorie target as a starting guide, not a rigid rule. If you're not seeing results after 2–3 weeks, adjust by 100–200 calories and reassess.

## FAQ — Calorie Calculator

**How accurate is the calorie calculator?**
The calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which is considered the most accurate formula for estimating BMR in the general population. Studies show it's accurate within 10% for most people. Real-world variation exists because individual metabolism, gut microbiome, and hormones all play a role.

**Do I need to count calories to lose weight?**
Not necessarily. Many people lose weight successfully without counting by eating whole foods, managing hunger with protein and fiber, and being mindful of portion sizes. However, tracking calories — even for just a few weeks — is one of the most effective ways to understand your eating patterns and identify where excess calories are coming from.

**How many calories should I eat to lose 1 kg per week?**
One kilogram of body fat contains roughly 7,700 calories. To lose 1 kg per week, you'd need a daily deficit of about 1,100 calories. For most people, this is aggressive and difficult to sustain. A more realistic target is 0.5 kg per week, which requires a 550-calorie daily deficit.

**Does the calculator work for teenagers?**
The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is validated for adults 18 and older. Teenagers have different nutritional needs due to growth and development. For under-18s, please consult a pediatric nutritionist rather than using adult calorie calculators.
